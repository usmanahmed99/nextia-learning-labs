## What one row means

One **task** is one customer conversation to run: instructions for the simulated customer (what they want, what they know, how they behave) and the expected actions (the tool calls a correct agent makes). The target is the database after those actions. One **recording** line is one conversation that an agent had on one task: every message, every tool call and result, the tokens and the model time.

## Why this dataset

It has everything an agent evaluation with side effects needs, under a licence that lets us show and redistribute traces: a written policy, tools that read and write a mock database, a simulated customer, a score from the final database, and the pass^k measure for repeated runs. Compared with the original τ-bench (MIT, but its README says its tasks are outdated), AppWorld (Apache-2.0 with a condition that its task data may only be redistributed encrypted) and ToolSandbox (a custom Apple licence). Full comparison: `reference/c10/m07/l01/DATASET-RESEARCH.md` in the course repository.

## Who made it

The database was generated by code that GPT-4 wrote and the authors corrected ("we create an example entry and use gpt-4 to generate a systematic code snippet to sample scalable entries, and manually polish minor bugs in the code", τ-bench paper, section 4). The authors wrote the task instructions and expected actions, and checked them with many runs of a GPT-4-turbo agent (more than 40 trials per retail task). τ³-bench fixed 26 retail tasks, mostly from τ-Bench Verified (Cuadron et al., SABER), reviewed with a team at Amazon. All people, emails and addresses are invented.

## Changes we made

None to the five files. We use a fixed subset of 15 test tasks (5, 9, 26, 27, 33, 55, 56, 61, 64, 71, 77, 94, 97, 101, 102), drawn with `random.Random(10).sample` from the 29 test tasks that have no model-judged assertions. The recordings are our own runs (gpt-6-luna and gpt-6.1-sol on Azure, 2026-10-09); the customer in them is gpt-6-luna playing the task's instructions.

## Limitations and cautions

- The customer is a model. When a conversation fails, the simulated customer may be the cause (for example, it does not follow its instruction to change its mind).
- 15 tasks and a few repeats are a small sample: differences of one or two tasks are not evidence.
- The database is invented and small; real orders, payments and customers are messier.
- Some tasks have open issues in the benchmark (task 5: the tools do not refuse a lookup before the user is authenticated).
- The policy checks in the notebook are ours, written in code from the policy text; the benchmark does not score them.
